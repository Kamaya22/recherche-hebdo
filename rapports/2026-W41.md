# Source diversity — Gravitational-wave astronomy (2026-W41)

## At a glance

This week produced 7 daily readings (day-1 through day-7) citing **30 total sources** drawn from **20 distinct venues** and **15 distinct publishers**. 19 of the 30 citations (63.3%) are open access, 7 (23.3%) are behind a paywall, and 4 (13.3%) have an unknown access status (mostly LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA collaboration papers in *Physical Review D* and *Classical and Quantum Gravity* where the specific article's access tier could not be confirmed). 22 of 30 (73.3%) are peer-reviewed; the remainder are institutional perspective pieces (LIGO/ESA/ESO public pages, Quanta Magazine) plus one genuine preprint. The single most notable finding: this is a narrowly disciplinary but well-triangulated week — nearly everything traces back to the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration's own publications or to a small number of specialist physics venues, which is appropriate for the topic but leaves the week more concentrated by publisher and by country than most others in the project so far.

## Publisher & venue concentration

No single publisher reached the 50% flagging threshold used elsewhere in this project, but the week was still fairly concentrated. By citation count: **Springer Nature** (Nature, Nature Reviews Physics, Living Reviews in Relativity) — 6 citations (20.0%); **American Physical Society** (Physical Review Letters, Physical Review X, Physical Review D) — 4 (13.3%); **IOP Publishing** (Classical and Quantum Gravity, direct) — 3 (10.0%); **IOP Publishing / SISSA Medialab** (Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics) — 3 (10.0%); **American Astronomical Society, published in partnership with IOP** (The Astrophysical Journal and its Supplement Series) — 2 (6.7%); **LIGO Scientific Collaboration** (public science summaries) — 2 (6.7%); **Cornell University** (arXiv) — 2 (6.7%). The remaining eight publishers (Caltech/MIT, Simons Foundation, AAAS, Annual Reviews, European Southern Observatory, Frontiers, MDPI, European Space Agency) each contributed exactly one citation.

If the three IOP-affiliated venues are grouped together (Classical and Quantum Gravity, JCAP, and the AAS/IOP-copublished *Astrophysical Journal* family), "content printed or co-printed by IOP Publishing" accounts for 8 of 30 citations (26.7%) — still below the flagging threshold, but a pattern worth watching if the project returns to gravitational-wave or particle-physics themes, since IOP is effectively the house publisher for this subfield's specialist journals. At the level of individual venues, *Classical and Quantum Gravity* and *JCAP* were this week's most-cited single venues, each appearing 3 times.

## Evidence mix

Of the 30 citations: 13 are primary studies (43.3%, mostly the LVK collaboration's own detection, catalogue, population, and GR-test papers), 9 are reviews (30.0%: the two Living Reviews in Relativity pieces, the Nature Reviews Physics roadmap, the JCAP Einstein Telescope science case, the Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics piece, and the two Frontiers/Galaxies mini-reviews), 7 are perspective pieces (23.3%: LIGO/ESA/ESO institutional pages, the Quanta Magazine article, and the Cosmic Explorer horizon study), and 1 is a preprint (3.3%: the GWTC-5.0 population-properties paper used on Day 3, explicitly flagged there as not yet peer-reviewed). This is a healthier review-to-primary-study ratio than a pure "primary research" week would give, which matters for a general-audience reading list: several of the heaviest technical results (the GWTC-3 population analysis, the GW170817 electromagnetic follow-up, the Einstein Telescope and next-generation-detector science cases) came filtered through a peer-reviewed synthesis rather than requiring readers to parse a raw collaboration paper directly. Only one preprint was used all week, and it was clearly labeled as such both in the day-3 text and in its citation record — no unflagged preprints entered the week's record.

## Open access & geography

19 of 30 citations (63.3%) are open access, close to the project's all-time average (69.7% per `STATS.md`, which itself lags one reading behind). The paywalled share (7 citations, 23.3%) is concentrated in a handful of venues — *Nature* (both appearances), the *Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics* piece, and the *Nature Reviews Physics* roadmap (cited on both Day 1 and Day 7) — all read via their abstract/introduction sections as the day-by-day readings specify, rather than requiring full paid access.

Geographically, publisher-country concentration was high this week: 14 of 30 citations (46.7%) trace to United States-headquartered publishers (APS, AAAS, Annual Reviews, Cornell/arXiv, the LIGO Laboratory and LIGO Scientific Collaboration, and the AAS) and 12 of 30 (40.0%) to United Kingdom-headquartered publishers (Springer Nature and the two IOP-affiliated venues), for a combined US+UK share of 86.7%. The remaining four citations split across Germany (ESO, 1), Switzerland (Frontiers and MDPI/Galaxies, 2), and France (the European Space Agency, 1). This is somewhat more US/UK-concentrated than the project's all-time figures (United States 37.5%, United Kingdom 36.1%, combined 73.6%), reflecting how much of gravitational-wave astronomy's institutional infrastructure (LIGO, APS, AAS) sits in the United States, with the dominant European physics-publishing houses (Springer Nature, IOP) based in the UK.

## Discipline & recency

Unsurprisingly for a single-topic physics week, discipline diversity was low by design: 20 of 30 citations (66.7%) are tagged `physics`, with the remainder split across `astrophysics` (4, 13.3%: the LIGO Laboratory and science-summary pages, plus the ESO press release), `astronomy` (3, 10.0%: the two AAS journal articles and the ESA page), and `multidisciplinary` (3, 10.0%: *Science* and the two *Nature* citations). This is expected and not a concern in itself — the project's other weeks cover different disciplines, and `STATS.md`'s all-time discipline distribution already shows `physics` as a meaningful but minority share (6.1%) of the full archive.

Recency spanned a genuinely wide range: from a 2011 *Living Reviews in Relativity* piece on interferometer design (still the standard reference, reflecting how much foundational detector theory predates the 2015 detection) through a 2026 preprint and science summary describing the newest catalogue release. The distribution skewed toward the last five years (14 of 30 citations, 46.7%, from 2021 or later) with a long tail of older foundational references (2011, 2016, 2017, 2018), which fits the pedagogical arc: early days leaned on foundational and historical sources, while the catalogue, methods, and synthesis days drew on the most current collaboration outputs.

## Blind spots & data quality

Three citations (10.0%) carry an `unknown` open-access status rather than a confirmed `open` or `paywall` tag: the 2016 and 2023 *Classical and Quantum Gravity* citations and the 2025 *Physical Review D* tests-of-general-relativity paper. All three are legitimate, clearly identified LVK collaboration publications; the `unknown` tag reflects genuine uncertainty about each specific article's access tier (collaboration papers in subscription journals sometimes carry individually negotiated open-access arrangements that are hard to confirm without direct access to the publisher's page) rather than any doubt about the source's existence or quality. No citation this week was tagged `to-verify` for discipline or country, and no new off-registry venues were introduced — every source_id used this week already existed in, or was added cleanly to, `registry.csv` (the only fully new venue added was `physical-review-d`; `jcap`, `arxiv`, and `esa-science` were reused from the existing registry). One source family that was notably absent all week: no national-academy or OECD/IPCC-style institutional report appeared, nor did any non-English-language source — both unsurprising for this topic, but worth naming as blind spots rather than leaving implicit.

## Recommendations

1. **Diversify beyond the IOP/Springer Nature/APS triangle** if a future physics-themed week returns to gravitational-wave or particle-physics territory: this week's venue list is thorough but narrow, and alternatives exist (e.g. *Reviews of Modern Physics*, *General Relativity and Gravitation*, or more institutional sources like NASA's or NSF's own public pages) that would pull the publisher mix away from the same handful of houses.
2. **Resolve the three `unknown` open-access tags** (the two *Classical and Quantum Gravity* citations and the *Physical Review D* GR-tests paper) by checking each article's specific access page directly — collaboration papers sometimes carry an individually funded open-access license that the general journal subscription model would otherwise obscure.
3. **Balance the US/UK publisher-country concentration** (86.7% this week vs. 73.6% all-time) in future physics or astronomy weeks by actively seeking out non-Anglophone institutional sources — ESA's French and German-language pages, Max Planck Institute releases, or JAXA/KAGRA's own Japanese-institution public communications (KAGRA itself went uncited this week despite being part of the detector network discussed on Days 2 and 6).
4. **Keep using the "flagged preprint" pattern** established on Day 3 and reused implicitly in spirit this week (no new preprints were needed) — it is a clean way to use the very newest collaboration results without overstating their certainty, and should remain the default whenever a catalogue or population paper is cited before its peer review completes.
5. **Consider a dedicated citation for KAGRA or Virgo's own institutional pages** in any future detector-methods week: this week's "perspective" category leaned entirely on LIGO's and ESA's public communications, even though Virgo (Italy/France, EGO consortium) and KAGRA (Japan) are co-equal partners in every catalogue and GR-test paper cited.
