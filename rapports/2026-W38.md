# Source diversity — Long COVID (2026-W38)

## At a glance

This week's seven readings cited **41 sources** across **22 distinct venues** and **17 distinct publishers** (counting each registry publisher string separately, e.g. treating "Elsevier," "Elsevier (The Lancet)," and "Elsevier (The Lancet Discovery Science)" as distinct imprints). **33 of 41 citations (80.5%) are confirmed open access**, 5 (12.2%) sit behind a paywall, and 3 (7.3%) have unconfirmed open-access status. **34 of 41 citations (82.9%) are peer-reviewed**; the 7 non-peer-reviewed citations are all institutional guidance or guideline documents (WHO's fact sheet, CDC's guidance pages, NICE's NG188, and today's National Academies report), not preprints — no preprint was cited this week at all, a clean result for a health-themed week. The single most notable feature of the week is how it closed: today's two readings (a National Academies consensus report and a CDC journal supplement) showed that the ME/CFS parallel this week traced informally since Day 1 has now become a formal institutional classification — "infection-associated chronic conditions" — that groups long COVID with ME/CFS, post-Lyme syndrome, and other post-viral illnesses under one umbrella.

## Publisher & venue concentration

No single publisher imprint crossed the 50% alert threshold, but the *Annals of Internal Medicine* (American College of Physicians) was this week's single most-cited venue with 5 citations (12.2%), reflecting how central the RECOVER cohort study and its editorial (Day 4) and the fluvoxamine/metformin trial (Day 6) were to the week's diagnostic and treatment story. More striking is the concentration once Elsevier's several imprints are combined: "Elsevier" (6 citations, via JACI and Brain, Behavior, and Immunity — Integrative), "Elsevier (The Lancet)" (2, via The Lancet Infectious Diseases), and "Elsevier (The Lancet Discovery Science)" (2, via eClinicalMedicine) together account for **10 of 41 citations (24.4%)** — below the alert threshold individually, but a real corporate concentration once its imprints are counted together, and one worth Kamil's attention since it spans four of this week's distinct topical threads (autoimmunity, ME/CFS history, psychological factors, and antiviral treatment). MDPI is the next most-used publisher (4 citations, 9.8%, via *International Journal of Molecular Sciences* and *Healthcare*), both fully open-access journals.

## Evidence mix

Of the 41 citations, **19 (46.3%) are reviews** — a high share, but expected for a week that spent four of seven days (2, 3, 4, 5) surveying competing mechanistic hypotheses and a diagnostic/historical literature that is itself dominated by review-level synthesis. **5 (12.2%) are guideline/institutional documents** (WHO, CDC, NICE, and today's NASEM report), reflecting how much of this week's diagnostic and controversy material rested on official case definitions rather than primary trials. **4 (9.8%) are cohort studies** (chiefly the RECOVER cohort, cited multiple times), **3 (7.3%) are RCTs** (RECOVER-VITAL/PAX LC, COVID-OUT, and the fluvoxamine/metformin trial), and **3 (7.3%) are meta-analyses**. Only **1 citation (2.4%) is tagged `to-verify` for evidence type** (a RECOVER program news page mentioned in Day 6's text, whose format did not cleanly fit the taxonomy). **No preprint was cited this week** — a genuinely clean result given the source-quality rules' strictness on unreviewed material, though it also means the week leaned entirely on the peer-reviewed and institutional literature even where that literature is still visibly unsettled (Days 4 and 5 both flagged real, unresolved scientific disagreement despite every source being reviewed or official).

## Open access & geography

Open-access share (80.5% confirmed open) is well above the project's all-time baseline (69.9% as of the last `STATS.md` generation) — helped by this being a health/public-policy theme where NIH-funded research (RECOVER), CDC and WHO institutional pages, and several fully open-access journals (*PLOS ONE*, *Infectious Diseases & Immunity*, *eClinicalMedicine*, *Healthcare*, *npj Primary Care Respiratory Medicine*) dominated. The 3 "unknown" citations are concentrated on Day 4 (the *Annals of Internal Medicine* editorial, whose open-access status could not be independently confirmed at the time of writing) and Day 6 (the RECOVER program news page).

Geographically, publisher headquarters this week skew heavily toward the United States (14 of 41 citations, 34.1%) and the United Kingdom (9, 22.0%), with Switzerland a notable third (8, 19.5%, via MDPI's Basel headquarters and Frontiers' Lausanne headquarters) and the Netherlands fourth (6, 14.6%, via Elsevier's Amsterdam headquarters). Two citations (4.9%, both citing *Molecular Diagnosis & Therapy*) carry a `to-verify` country tag inherited from the registry. Every institutional source cited this week — WHO (Geneva), CDC (Atlanta), NICE (London), and today's NASEM (Washington, DC) — is a government or intergovernmental body from the US or Europe; no institutional or academic source from Asia, Latin America, Africa, or Oceania was cited at any point in the week, a gap worth naming given that long COVID's societal and disability stakes (Day 6) are global.

## Discipline & recency

Discipline is dominated by general-medicine (13 of 41 citations, 31.7% — driven by *Annals of Internal Medicine* and several general-medicine-classified journals), followed by public-health (5, 12.2%, largely WHO/CDC institutional pages) and infectious-disease (5, 12.2%). Smaller but topically important slices include microbiology (4, 9.8%), immunology (3, 7.3%, via JACI), neurology (3, 7.3%), and psychoneuroimmunology (2, 4.9%, via *Brain, Behavior, and Immunity — Integrative*). No citation this week came from a dedicated psychiatry or psychology-specialist journal, despite Day 5's substantial discussion of the biomedical-versus-psychosomatic debate and the Engelmann et al. meta-analysis on depression/anxiety — that study was published in the general-clinical journal *eClinicalMedicine* rather than a psychiatry venue. On recency, the week is weighted toward current material: 14 citations (34.1%) are from 2025 and 12 (29.3%) from 2024, with 3 (7.3%) already from 2026 (the RECOVER-VITAL trial results and the fluvoxamine/metformin trial) — appropriate for a fast-moving clinical research area, though it leaves comparatively little room for older foundational material (only 3 citations, 7.3%, predate 2023, all tied to the 2021 NICE guideline and its critique).

## Blind spots & data quality

Two citations of *Molecular Diagnosis & Therapy* carry a `to-verify` country tag in `registry.csv` (Adis-imprint journals have historically been edited from New Zealand/Australia but are now Springer Nature UK-registered — worth Kamil's resolution). One citation (the RECOVER program's own news page, Day 6) is tagged `to-verify` for evidence type, since a program-milestones page does not fit cleanly into the review/study/guideline taxonomy. A labeling nuance worth flagging explicitly: today's National Academies report is tagged `peer_reviewed: false` per this project's existing convention for institutional guidance documents (matching how the WHO fact sheet and NICE guideline are tagged) — but NASEM consensus reports do go through the National Academies' own formal, independent report-review process before release, which is a more rigorous check than a simple institutional fact sheet gets. The taxonomy in `data/README.md` does not currently distinguish "reviewed by an independent expert committee" from "not reviewed at all," which this week's citation is the first to surface as a real edge case. No citation this week was off-registry, and, as noted above, no source outside the US, UK, Switzerland, and the Netherlands was cited at any point in the week.

While auditing this week's data, a pre-existing formatting bug was found and fixed in `registry.csv`: the `national-academies-press` row's `name` field ("National Academies Press (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine)") contained unquoted commas, which silently misaligned that row's publisher/discipline/country columns for anyone parsing the file with a standard CSV reader (including `tools/build_stats.py`). It has been corrected to a quoted field; no other row was affected, and no citation counts changed as a result.

## Recommendations

1. **Watch the combined Elsevier-imprint share (24.4% this week).** No single imprint crossed the alert threshold, but "Elsevier," "The Lancet," and "eClinicalMedicine" together carried four distinct topical threads this week. A future long-COVID-adjacent week could deliberately pull at least one contested claim (autoimmunity, or the psychological-factors debate) from a non-Elsevier venue for independent editorial perspective.
2. **Seek a non-Western institutional or academic source when the topic allows it.** Every institutional citation this week (WHO, CDC, NICE, NASEM) was a US or European body, and no academic source from Asia, Latin America, Africa, or Oceania was cited — notable for a condition the week itself described as a "global" ~$1 trillion/year burden.
3. **Resolve the `molecular-diagnosis-therapy` country tag** in `registry.csv` — it is now cited across two separate weeks and remains `to-verify`.
4. **Consider refining the `peer_reviewed` taxonomy** in `data/README.md` to distinguish a National-Academies-style independent-committee-reviewed report from an un-reviewed institutional fact sheet — both currently collapse to `peer_reviewed: false`, which understates the evidentiary weight of a NASEM consensus report relative to, say, a plain agency web page.
5. **Look for a dedicated ME/CFS-specialist or psychiatry venue in future related weeks**, now that the CDC's IACCI framework formally links long COVID and ME/CFS research; this week's ME/CFS material (Day 5) arrived entirely through general-medicine and multidisciplinary venues rather than a specialist source.
