# Lithium-ion battery science and the energy storage transition

Lithium-ion batteries are the quiet infrastructure behind two of the biggest technological shifts of this decade: the electrification of transport and the build-out of renewable electricity grids. Understanding how they actually work — and where they fall short — is necessary to make sense of debates about electric-vehicle range, grid blackouts, mineral supply chains, and the much-discussed "what comes after lithium." The field sits at the intersection of chemistry, materials science, industrial policy, and geopolitics, which makes it a useful lens for thinking about how a laboratory discovery becomes a trillion-dollar industry.

It also raises genuinely open scientific and policy questions: battery safety and thermal runaway remain engineering challenges even in mature designs; the recycling industry needed to close the material loop is still nascent; and the mining of lithium, cobalt, and nickel carries real environmental and human costs that are unevenly distributed across producing countries. At the same time, alternative chemistries (sodium-ion, solid-state, iron-air) are maturing fast enough that "lithium-ion" may not remain the default answer for every use case.

## Tentative 7-day outline

1. **Day 1 — Panorama.** What a lithium-ion cell is made of, why this chemistry won out over alternatives, and why it became central to the energy transition.
2. **Day 2 — Mechanisms.** The electrochemistry of charge/discharge: intercalation, the solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI), and why cells degrade over time.
3. **Day 3 — Safety.** Thermal runaway, failure modes, and the engineering and chemistry fixes that have made batteries dramatically safer over the past decade.
4. **Day 4 — Supply chains and critical minerals.** Lithium, cobalt, nickel: where they come from, mining's environmental and social footprint, and geopolitical concentration of refining.
5. **Day 5 — Recycling and the circular economy.** End-of-life challenges, current recycling technologies, and the policy push to close the material loop.
6. **Day 6 — Beyond lithium-ion.** Sodium-ion, solid-state, LFP's rise, and other chemistries competing for grid-scale and EV markets; controversies and realistic timelines.
7. **Day 7 — Synthesis.** What's settled vs. still open, big unresolved questions, and a weekly source-diversity report.

This outline is a guide, not a contract — later days may shift based on what the research turns up.
