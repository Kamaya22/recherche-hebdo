# Long COVID — the biology of post-viral illness, diagnostic challenges, and what treatments are being tested

## Why this theme matters

Five years after the acute phase of the COVID-19 pandemic subsided in most countries, long COVID remains one of the largest unresolved problems in modern medicine: tens of millions of people worldwide report symptoms — fatigue, cognitive impairment ("brain fog"), post-exertional malaise, cardiovascular and autonomic dysfunction — that persist for months or years after the initial infection. It is also a uniquely instructive case study in how biomedicine handles a novel, contested, multi-system illness in real time: competing mechanistic hypotheses (viral persistence, immune dysregulation, autoimmunity, microclotting, vagal/autonomic nervous system damage) are being tested simultaneously, no validated biomarker or diagnostic test yet exists, and the condition has reopened long-standing tensions — familiar from ME/CFS research — between biomedical and psychosomatic framings of post-viral illness. Long COVID also sits at the intersection of basic science and urgent public-health need: healthcare systems, disability insurers, and workplaces are already making decisions under deep uncertainty about prevalence, prognosis, and treatment. Understanding where the science is solid, where it is contested, and what is actually being tested in clinical trials is therefore valuable well beyond COVID itself — it is a template for how we study, diagnose, and eventually treat post-infectious chronic illness in general, including conditions like post-Lyme syndrome and post-viral ME/CFS that long predate the pandemic.

## Tentative 7-day outline

- **Day 1 — Panorama**: What long COVID is (working definitions from WHO/CDC/NICE), how common it is, the core symptom clusters, and why it has been so hard to pin down as a single disease entity.
- **Day 2 — Mechanisms I**: Leading biological hypotheses — viral persistence/reservoirs, immune dysregulation and autoimmunity, endothelial dysfunction and microclotting.
- **Day 3 — Mechanisms II**: The neurological and autonomic angle — brain fog, dysautonomia/POTS, the gut-brain and vagus-nerve hypotheses, and overlap with ME/CFS.
- **Day 4 — Diagnostic challenges**: Why there is still no validated biomarker or test, candidate biomarkers under study, and the practical/clinical consequences of diagnosing a syndrome by exclusion.
- **Day 5 — Controversies and open debates**: The biomedical-vs-psychosomatic framing debate, definitional disagreements across agencies, and lessons (and warnings) from the ME/CFS research history.
- **Day 6 — Treatments and applications**: What is actually being tested in clinical trials (antivirals, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, immunomodulators, rehabilitation approaches), and the societal implications — disability systems, return-to-work, healthcare costs.
- **Day 7 — Synthesis**: What is settled vs. still open, the week's big open questions, and pointers for going deeper.

This outline is a guide, not a contract — later days will be adjusted based on what the day-to-day research turns up.
