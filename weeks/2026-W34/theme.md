# The biology of aging

Aging is the single largest risk factor for the diseases that kill and disable most people in wealthy countries — heart disease, cancer, dementia, type 2 diabetes — yet for most of medical history it has been treated as an unavoidable backdrop rather than a process with its own biology. Over the past two decades, geroscience has reframed aging as a set of interconnected, measurable, and in some cases modifiable molecular and cellular mechanisms, rather than simple wear-and-tear. This matters enormously for public health: as populations worldwide age, the gap between lifespan (years lived) and healthspan (years lived in good health) is widening, and understanding the mechanisms of aging is central to closing it. The field is also scientifically exciting because it sits at the intersection of evolutionary biology, molecular biology, and clinical medicine, and because a handful of interventions first discovered in worms and mice are now being tested in humans. It is also genuinely contested terrain: whether aging should be classified as a disease, which mechanisms are causal versus correlative, and which interventions are ready for clinical translation are all live debates.

## Tentative 7-day outline

- **Day 1 (overview)**: What aging is biologically, the "hallmarks of aging" framework, and why the field matters for healthspan vs. lifespan.
- **Day 2**: Cellular senescence — the biology of "zombie cells," how they accumulate, and their role in age-related disease.
- **Day 3**: Genomic and epigenetic mechanisms — telomere attrition, DNA damage, and epigenetic clocks as measures of biological age.
- **Day 4**: Mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolism, and nutrient-sensing pathways (caloric restriction, mTOR, AMPK) — mechanisms and evolutionary logic.
- **Day 5**: Interventions and their evidence base — rapamycin, metformin, senolytics, and what human trial data actually show (and don't).
- **Day 6**: Controversies and open debates — is aging a disease, programmed vs. damage theories, and the limits of animal-model translation.
- **Day 7 (synthesis)**: Tying the week together — what is settled vs. open, biggest unresolved questions, and pointers for going deeper.

The outline is a guide, not a contract — later days will adjust based on what the research surfaces.
