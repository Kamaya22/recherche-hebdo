# Week theme: Solar geoengineering

**Solar geoengineering — the science, risks, and governance debates around deliberately cooling the planet**

As greenhouse-gas emissions keep rising faster than they are being cut, a once-fringe idea has moved into mainstream scientific and policy discussion: deliberately reflecting a fraction of incoming sunlight back to space to cool the Earth. Proposals range from injecting reflective aerosols into the stratosphere to brightening marine clouds, and funding for research — and for a handful of small outdoor experiments — has surged since 2023. The idea is scientifically serious (backed by volcanic-eruption analogues and detailed climate models) and deeply contested: it does nothing about ocean acidification or the root cause of warming, its side effects on regional rainfall and ozone are still poorly constrained, and no international body currently governs who could deploy it, at what scale, or how it could be stopped. This week traces the physics, the evidence base, the live controversies, and the emerging governance debate around a technology that could plausibly be tested at meaningful scale within this decade.

**Tentative 7-day outline** (a guide, adjusted as the week's research develops):
- **Day 1 — Panorama**: what solar geoengineering is, the three leading proposed methods, why it is being taken seriously now, and why it remains controversial.
- **Day 2 — Mechanisms**: how stratospheric aerosol injection would work physically, drawing on the volcanic-eruption analogy and aerosol microphysics.
- **Day 3 — Key findings**: what climate models project about cooling potential and side effects (precipitation shifts, monsoon disruption, stratospheric ozone).
- **Day 4 — State of the art**: current funding, field experiments, and who is actually doing the work today.
- **Day 5 — Controversies and open debates**: termination shock, moral hazard, unilateral deployment, and environmental-justice concerns.
- **Day 6 — Methods, how we know**: the limits of modeling and small-scale outdoor experiments, and what real-world monitoring would require.
- **Day 7 — Synthesis**: governance proposals on the table, what is scientifically settled versus genuinely open, and pointers for going deeper.
